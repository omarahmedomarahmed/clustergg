## 1. Verdict table

| Q | Short answer | Confidence | What decides it | Blocks the business? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1 — Does Discord permit this?** | **Probably not as built.** Developer Policy §6 forbids messaging that "may not contain material unrelated to an Application's function." A third-party brand's paid creative inside a bot-posted card is the paradigm case of unrelated material. Cash-convertible points for engagement is separately exposed under §13 (manipulating engagement) and the App Directory's "gambling-adjacent or addictive behavior" bar. | **Needs counsel** on §6; **Contested** on whether "relevant to the function" can be read to cover a sponsor's creative in a product whose stated function *is* sponsored challenges. | Whether a reviewer reads "the function of the Application" as *what the app does for the user* (ads are unrelated → prohibited) or *what the app is* (an ad-funded challenges platform → ads are its function). Discord publishes no interpretive gloss. | **Yes, for the paid-creative half.** The sponsored-challenge half plausibly survives; see §4. |
| **Q2 — Is paying cash for engagement regulated?** | **Federally: likely not an MSB on these facts** — ClusterGG pays its own obligation to its own users and does not accept value from one person to transmit to another. **State: likely outside MTL** — the CSBS model act's "stored value" expressly excludes value "not sold to the public but issued and distributed as part of a loyalty, rewards, or promotional program." **OFAC applies regardless.** **IRS: the $600 in the code is stale — the threshold is $2,000 for 2026.** | `Settled` on the 1099 threshold and on OFAC applying. `Needs counsel` on FinCEN administrator status and on the brand-money-through-to-gamer characterisation. | Whether the brand's $350 makes ClusterGG a conduit ("receiving money for transmission" from brand to gamer) or a principal paying its own prize obligation out of its own revenue. | **No** — but two live defects: a stale IRS threshold and a country block-list that is out of date in both directions. |
| **Q3 — "Directed to children"? Is 16 right?** | **Not "directed to children" under COPPA on the facts as stated — but almost certainly "mixed audience," and almost certainly in scope of the UK code.** 16 is defensible as a *ceiling-of-caution* line for GDPR Art. 8 but is **not** the operative line for any one regime: COPPA is 13, the UK code is *under 18*, and GDPR Art. 8 is 16 only where a member state has not lowered it. | `Contested` on COPPA classification; `Settled` that the UK code applies to under-18s and that the DSA bars profiling-based ads to known minors. | Whether "child-oriented activities and incentives" (16 CFR 312.2) captures a points-and-prizes loop on a gaming platform, and what the actual audience data shows. | **No.** But "earning is off under 16" does **not** remove exposure: COPPA and the UK code attach to *collection and design*, not to whether money moves. |
| **Q4 — May an unredeemed trophy expire?** | **Probably yes, with disclosure — and the "never expires" choice is the one creating exposure, not solving it.** Federal gift-card law does not reach an award not sold for payment. Delaware unclaimed-property law is the sharp edge: the "loyalty card" and "game-related digital content" carve-outs **both expressly exclude anything redeemable for money**, which is exactly what a trophy is. | `Contested`. The federal analysis is close to `Settled`; the escheat analysis is `Needs counsel`. | Whether a trophy is "a fixed and certain interest in intangible property … owed" (12 Del. C. §1130(21)) — i.e. whether the gamer has a matured claim or only a revocable promise. | **No** — but "never expires" plus Delaware incorporation is a reporting obligation nobody has scoped. See `lib/breakage.ts`. |

If the trophy is a revocable promise rather than a matured obligation → not property at all. | same · 2026-08-08 |
| **12 Del. C. §1133(14)** | "**A stored-value card or gift card, 5 years** after the later of the date of purchase, the addition of funds …, a verification of the balance by the owner, or the last indication of interest in the property. For a stored-value card or gift card, the amount unclaimed is the amount representing the maximum cost to the issuer of the merchandise, goods…" | If a trophy is characterised as stored value, the dormancy clock is **five years from last indication of interest** — and "never expires" does not stop that clock. It only guarantees the balance is still there when the clock runs out. | **Against "never expires."** A perpetual liability does not avoid escheat; it preserves the thing that escheats. | same · 2026-08-08 |
| **12 Del. C. §1133(6)** | "**Money or credits owed to a customer as a result of a retail business transaction, 5 years** after the obligation arose." | The catch-all a Delaware auditor would reach for if the stored-value characterisation failed. Also five years. | **Against.** Both routes converge on five years. | same · 2026-08-08 |

> **The finding `lib/breakage.ts` half-anticipated:** that file says an expiry "is how a platform ends up with an escheatment problem it never discussed." The sources say the reverse is at least as true — **under Delaware law, never expiring does not avoid escheatment; it just means the state, not the gamer and not ClusterGG, is the likely eventual recipient.** The file's decision never to bank breakage as revenue is unaffected by this and remains the conservative choice. What is missing is not an expiry policy but a *dormancy and reporting* policy.

**What I could not find for Q4:** whether any state has addressed a *skill-contest prize* held in an unredeemed form (as opposed to a purchased gift card); the RUUPA (2016) official text and comments, which I did not retrieve — the Delaware provisions above are RUUPA-derived but Delaware's variations were not diffed against the uniform act; and any analysis of non-US unclaimed-property equivalents for our MENA/EU population.

| **Q1.** Does Developer Policy §6 prohibit a sponsor's creative on a bot-posted card? | "The function of the Application" means what the bot does *for the user* — profiles, challenges, leaderboards. There is no acceptance-from-one-person-and-transmission-to-another, which the regulation requires. | **The no-P2P-transfer fact is the strongest fact ClusterGG has here, and it should lead the memo to counsel.** Also decisive: whether CP is ever quoted, traded or accepted anywhere outside the product. | Counsel, with a FinCEN administrative ruling request as the definitive route if the answer matters enough (FIN-2019-G001 §6 describes the process). |

### 7.6 MENA — filled in, country by country

**The regional rule, stated once.** Across the Arab world the near-universal position is that **gambling is prohibited**, and the prohibition is defined by two elements that recur almost verbatim from statute to statute: **(1) chance decides the outcome, and (2) each party stakes something**. ClusterGG's challenges are skill-based and free to enter, so on the face of these definitions they fail *both* limbs — which is the favourable reading. What survives the gambling analysis is a **separate and independent licensing layer**: several states require a permit for *any* commercial promotion or competition, chance or not.

### 7.11 The age floors, consolidated

The single change that would move the most jurisdictions at once is not raising the earning age; it is **not showing profiled advertising to anyone under 18, anywhere**.
